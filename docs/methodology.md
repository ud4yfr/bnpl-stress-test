# Methodology

The question is: **How much annualized credit-loss pressure could current earnings capacity absorb under defined accounting, exposure, and attribution assumptions?**

The default is a deterministic company-level accounting scenario. For each company, reported operating income/profit and recognized provision share a consolidated-company scope with the exposure denominator. Pre-credit-loss operating income is operating income plus provision. Quarterly capacity is that amount divided by average exposure; static annualized capacity is four times quarterly capacity. This is an accounting capacity measure, not cash profit, forecast, or probability of failure.

Affirm's average LHI is directly disclosed. Klarna's average gross consumer receivables is calculated from its Q1 opening and closing gross balances. Both periods end March 31, 2026 and are in USD. Klarna's Fair Financing write-offs are retained as a product-level observed fact, but no corresponding recovery disclosure was found; therefore no comparable product-level NCO rate or product-level margin is calculated.

Affirm reports under U.S. GAAP and Klarna under IFRS. The main comparison uses each firm's reported operating result (not an adjusted/non-IFRS measure); this remains an accounting-framework limitation, rather than a claim that GAAP and IFRS are identical.

Loss-rate scenarios are analyst-configured annualized rates. Revenue, funding-cost, and operating-expense stresses affect the available accounting capacity. The analysis intentionally does not fit a historical distribution or report P(loss): the available data does not support that claim.
